Frequently Asked Pharmacology Questions

Key insights on active salts, brand names, and weight-based calculation.

What is the difference between a Brand Name and an Active Chemical Salt?

The active drug salt (e.g., Paracetamol) is the therapeutic molecule that lowers fever or stops pain. The brand name (e.g., Dolo 650, Crocin, Calpol) is the commercial trademark under which different companies sell that exact chemical salt. HIMKIV MedGuide maps brand names to active salts so you avoid double-dosing identical molecules.

Why must pediatric doses be calculated by body weight rather than age?

Two 5-year-old children can have very different weights (e.g., 14 kg vs. 24 kg). Drug distribution and liver clearance depend directly on body weight, so dosing purely by age causes severe under-treatment or dangerous toxicity. Clinical pharmacologists calculate pediatric posology in mg per kg.

How does the clinical survey protect against drug allergy reactions?

In Step 2 of the survey, you specify any known hypersensitivities (such as Penicillin or NSAIDs). The calculation engine flags any cross-reacting compounds (such as Amoxicillin) in red with severe warnings and automatically elevates safe alternatives (such as Azithromycin).
