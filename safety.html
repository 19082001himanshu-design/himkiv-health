Clinical Stewardship & Poison Prevention

Medication Safety & Emergency Protocols

Critical pharmacological standards designed to prevent accidental pediatric overdose, acute liver necrosis, superbug antibiotic resistance, and delayed emergency room triage.

Immediate Emergency Triage: When to Call 112 / 911

Never delay emergency medical evaluation if experiencing any of these acute signs:

Crushing Chest Pain: Radiating to left shoulder, arm, neck, or jaw accompanied by cold clamminess (Rule out Heart Attack).
Anaphylaxis: Rapid swelling of lips, tongue, or throat with stridor breathing or hives after starting a new medicine.
Thunderclap Headache: Sudden explosive headache reaching maximal intensity in seconds, or headache with facial droop/weakness.
Pediatric Lethargy & Dehydration: Child unable to stay awake, sunken eyes, no wet diapers for >8 hours, or fever with stiff neck.
1

Acetaminophen (Paracetamol) Safe Ceiling: Max 4000 mg / 24 Hours

Hepatic toxicity prevention standard

Paracetamol is metabolized in the liver. At standard therapeutic doses, the toxic metabolite N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine (NAPQI) is rapidly conjugated with glutathione. When the daily ceiling of 4000 mg is exceeded (or >60 mg/kg in children), glutathione stores deplete, causing direct centrilobular hepatic necrosis.

Key Safety Rule: Beware of "Hidden Paracetamol" in over-the-counter multi-symptom cold and flu syrups (e.g. cold capsules containing paracetamol + phenylephrine + chlorpheniramine). Taking a 650mg tablet while drinking cold syrup can easily push you over the 4g daily threshold.
2

Antibiotic Resistance Stewardship: Complete the Entire Prescribed Duration

Preventing multidrug-resistant superbugs

Antibiotics (e.g., Amoxicillin-Clavulanate, Cefixime, Azithromycin) eliminate bacteria over a specific pharmacodynamic timeline. Stopping treatment on Day 3 because you "feel better" leaves behind the strongest, partially mutated bacteria, which multiply and become resistant to that entire antibiotic class.

Key Safety Rule: Antibiotics do not work against viral infections like seasonal influenza, common colds, or Covid-19. Never demand antibiotics for viral fevers, and never reuse leftover antibiotics from a past illness.
3

Pediatric Posology: Always Dose by Body Weight, Never by Spoon

Zero-error pediatric liquid posology

Standard kitchen teaspoons vary in volume from 3 ml to 8 ml. A 50% dosing error in a 12 kg toddler can lead to dangerous hypothermia, acute kidney distress, or under-treatment. Always check the concentration label (e.g., 120 mg / 5 ml vs 250 mg / 5 ml) and calculate exact milliliters using calibrated syringes.

Key Safety Rule: Always shake liquid suspensions well before drawing the dose, as active drug crystals settle at the bottom of the bottle.
4

NSAID Safety: Protect Your Stomach, Kidneys, and Pregnancy

Ibuprofen, Diclofenac & Naproxen guidelines

NSAIDs non-selectively inhibit COX-1 and COX-2 enzymes, reducing protective gastric prostaglandins and renal blood flow. They must always be taken with food or milk, and are strictly contraindicated in active peptic ulcers, chronic kidney disease, and the third trimester of pregnancy.

Key Safety Rule: Never give Aspirin or NSAIDs to children or teenagers recovering from viral infections like varicella (chickenpox) or influenza due to the risk of Reye's Syndrome, a fatal hepatic encephalopathy.
